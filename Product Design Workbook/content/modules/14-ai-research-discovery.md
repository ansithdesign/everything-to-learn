Research is where AI is both most useful and most dangerous. Useful, because the slowest parts of research (transcribing, tagging, searching hours of recordings, writing it up) are exactly what language models do well. Dangerous, because the valuable part (hearing what a real person means, and noticing what they didn't say) is what models imitate without doing.

This chapter explains the fundamentals of AI-assisted research and where practice stands in 2026. For research methods themselves see [[ch:04]].

:::pull
Use AI to handle the volume of evidence. Never use it to invent the evidence.
:::

## 1. The research workflow, stage by stage

| Stage | AI is good at | Keep human |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Plan** | Drafting research questions, screeners, interview guides, survey wording; spotting leading questions | Deciding what's worth learning; ethics and consent |
| **Recruit and schedule** | Screening responses, scheduling, reminders | Judging who is actually representative |
| **Collect** | Transcription, translation, note-taking, auto-summaries, AI-moderated interviews | Live moderation of usability tests; rapport; sensitive topics |
| **Analyze** | First-pass tagging, clustering, quote retrieval, cross-interview search | Deciding what the patterns *mean*; what's missing |
| **Share** | Draft reports, highlight reels, stakeholder-specific summaries | The recommendation and the confidence you attach to it |

## 2. Tools and categories (October 2026)

Vendor guides describe a common stack: an AI-moderated interview platform for conversations at scale, a repository with AI analysis for storage and synthesis, and a recruiting or screening tool. Dovetail is consistently described as the repository and synthesis layer; Outset, Wondering, Listen Labs, Usercall and Koji are named in the AI-interviewer category.[[ref:cleverx-playbook]] Nearly all of this coverage is vendor-written, I found no independent head-to-head test of synthesis quality, and prices differ between sources, so check each tool's own page.

Researchers themselves are cautious. The User Interviews 2026 State of User Research, as relayed by a vendor blog, reports 80 percent of researchers using AI (up 24 points in a year) and 91 percent worried about hallucination.[[ref:cleverx-playbook]] NN/g's State of UX 2026 is reported to put researcher AI adoption near 69 percent;[[ref:nng-state-ux-2026]] the two figures come from different surveys and samples, so don't blend them.

## 3. Synthesis with AI, done safely

AI synthesis fails in predictable ways: it over-weights what's repeated and fluent, flattens disagreement, loses context, and can fabricate a quote that sounds right. A safe process:

1. **Keep the raw material the source of truth.** Every claim must link to a timestamp or transcript line.
2. **Ask for evidence, not conclusions.** "List every passage about onboarding, with speaker and timestamp" beats "what are the insights?"
3. **Run it twice** with different prompts or models; the differences show you what's fragile.
4. **Spot-check quotes** against the transcript. Check at least ten, every time.
5. **Do the interpretive pass yourself.** Cluster by hand or review the AI clusters critically, and rename them in your own words.
6. **Mark confidence.** Say what was observed, what was inferred and what's unknown.

:::framework Evidence trace
For each insight, write: **Claim** (one sentence), **Evidence** (quotes or observations, with source and timestamp), **Counter-evidence** (who said or did the opposite), **Confidence** (high, medium or low and why), **Generated by** (you, AI-drafted and checked, or AI only). If the last answer is "AI only," it is not an insight yet.
:::

:::warning Common trap: the summary replaces the listening
If you only read the summary, you lose the hesitations, contradictions and surprises that produce the best ideas. Watch or read a sample of sessions in full. NN/g's position, as relayed by secondary coverage, is that AI cannot "watch" what a user does, so usability moderation stays human.
:::

## 4. Synthetic users and AI personas

Synthetic users are language-model "participants" you can interview instantly. NN/g re-ran three studies with ChatGPT and a synthetic-user product and found the responses overly agreeable and shallow.[[ref:nng-synthetic-users]] That matches how models work: they produce the *typical* answer, which is exactly what research is trying to get past.

Where they may still help: rehearsing an interview guide, brainstorming questions, stress-testing a screener, or role-playing a stakeholder before a presentation. Where they must not be used: as evidence for a decision, as a replacement for talking to users, or in a report without a clear label. Label any such output "synthetic, unvalidated."

:::decision A rule for synthetic participants
Use them to improve the *questions*. Never use them to supply the *answers*.
:::

## 5. AI-moderated interviews

AI interviewers can run many conversations at once, in many languages, at any hour, and follow up on answers. Their strengths are breadth and speed for well-defined questions (concept reactions, satisfaction drivers, simple behaviors). Their weaknesses are the ones that matter in discovery: they struggle with silence, emotion, context in the room and the unexpected tangent.

Before you use one:

- **Disclose** to participants that they are talking to an AI, and get consent for recording and data use. Rules on this are tightening (see [[ch:22]]).
- **Pilot** with five people and read every transcript for odd probes or leading follow-ups.
- **Pair it** with human interviews for the open-ended work.
- **Check** where the data is stored and whether it trains models.

## 6. Quantitative and behavioral data

- **Survey analysis:** open-text coding at scale is a strong use case. Spot-check categories and keep the codebook.
- **Analytics questions:** natural-language querying of product data can speed exploration but can silently ask the wrong question. Ask it to show the query or logic, and validate against a known number.
- **Session recordings:** tools now summarize or flag friction. Treat flags as leads to review, not findings.
- **Support tickets and reviews:** good for mining themes; beware of the loudest voices being over-represented.

## 7. Using AI to prepare better, not just analyze faster

- Draft a research plan, then ask the model to find the weakest assumption in it.
- Ask it to rewrite your interview questions to remove leading wording, then compare with the Mom Test principle (ask about past behavior, not opinions).[[ref:mom-test]]
- Have it play a skeptical PM and list objections to your findings before you present them.
- Generate a one-page brief for stakeholders from your notes, then correct it against your evidence trace.

:::example A hypothetical synthesis check
You ask a tool to summarize twelve interviews. It says "users find the approval step confusing." You ask for every passage on approval. Seven participants mention it; three call it confusing, two call it reassuring, two are neutral. The summary reflected the loudest voices. Your finding becomes: "Approval confuses people who approve rarely and reassures frequent approvers," with those quotes attached.
:::

:::exercise Try it: audit an AI summary (2 hours)
Take three real interviews you ran. Ask a tool for themes. Then check every quote against the transcript, count how many participants actually support each theme, and list anything important it missed. Record the error rate and use it to decide what you'll trust the tool for.
:::

## Quick check

:::check Knowledge check 1
A tool produces five tidy themes from forty interviews. What do you do before sharing them?
---
Trace each theme to specific quotes and participants, check quotes against transcripts, look for counter-evidence and missing segments, read a sample of sessions yourself, and label confidence and what was AI-generated.
:::

:::check Knowledge check 2
A PM proposes testing a concept with AI personas instead of real users to save time. How do you respond?
---
Synthetic respondents give typical, agreeable answers and cannot show real behavior, so they can't validate a decision. Offer a cheap real alternative (five quick interviews or an unmoderated test), and use the personas only to sharpen questions.
:::
